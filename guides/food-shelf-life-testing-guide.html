Food Shelf-Life Testing: Methods, Costs & How to Set a Best-Before Date

Real-time vs accelerated shelf-life studies, what's tested, costs and a step-by-step plan for food brands.

By the Lab.Works Editorial Team · Updated September 2026 · Editorial policy

A defensible shelf life protects consumers and your brand. It must be based on evidence — microbiological, chemical and sensory.

Real-time vs accelerated

  • Real-time: store under intended conditions and test at intervals until the product fails. Most reliable.
  • Accelerated (ASLT): elevated temperature/humidity speed up degradation; useful for shelf-stable products when the degradation mechanism is understood.

What's measured

  • Microbiology: total plate count, yeast & mould, relevant pathogens
  • Chemistry: pH, water activity, rancidity (peroxide value), vitamin loss
  • Sensory: appearance, odour, flavour, texture

Planning your study

  1. Define failure criteria before you start.
  2. Choose time points (e.g. 0, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%, 125% of target life).
  3. Test samples from production — not kitchen prototypes.
  4. Add a safety margin to the final date.
Studies commonly cost from about US$500 to several thousand. Get quotes from food labs.
