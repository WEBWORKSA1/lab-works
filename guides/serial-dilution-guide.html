Serial Dilutions Made Simple: Plate Counts, Standard Curves & ELISAs

Understand serial dilution math, choose the right fold, and avoid the pipetting errors that ruin standard curves.

By the Lab.Works Editorial Team · Updated September 2026 · Editorial policy

A serial dilution repeatedly dilutes a sample by a fixed factor, producing a geometric series of concentrations.

The math

Cₙ = C₀ / Fⁿ

With a 10-fold series, tube 1 is 1:10, tube 2 1:100, tube 3 1:1,000. With 2-fold, concentrations halve each step — ideal for standard curves and MIC assays.

Choosing a fold

  • 10-fold: bacterial and viral titres spanning many logs.
  • 2- or 3-fold: standard curves, ELISAs, antibody titrations.

Technique checklist

  1. Pre-fill all tubes with diluent.
  2. Mix each tube thoroughly (vortex or pipette up and down 5–10×).
  3. Change tips between every transfer.
  4. Label everything before starting.
