Lab Safety Essentials: The Rules Every Lab Should Enforce

PPE, chemical storage, SDS, waste, biosafety levels and emergency procedures in one practical checklist.

By the Lab.Works Editorial Team · Updated September 2026 · Editorial policy

Most lab incidents are preventable with consistent basics.

Personal protective equipment

  • Lab coat, safety glasses/goggles and closed-toe shoes at all times
  • Gloves matched to the chemical (nitrile is not universal — check compatibility charts)
  • Face shields for splash hazards; cryo-gloves for liquid nitrogen

Chemical management

  • Keep a current Safety Data Sheet (SDS) for every chemical
  • Segregate incompatibles: acids from bases, oxidisers from flammables
  • Label secondary containers with name, hazard and date

Biosafety levels

BSLExamplesKey controls
1Non-pathogenic E. coliStandard practices
2Staphylococcus aureus, human cell linesBSC for aerosols, restricted access
3M. tuberculosisNegative pressure, respirators
4Ebola virusPositive-pressure suits, isolated facility

Emergencies

Know the location of eyewash stations, safety showers, spill kits, fire extinguishers and exits. Report every incident and near-miss.
