Home & Well Water Testing: What to Test, When and How

Which contaminants to test in tap and private well water, how often, how to sample, and how to read your results.

By the Lab.Works Editorial Team · Updated September 2026 · Editorial policy

Public water systems are monitored by utilities, but private wells are the owner's responsibility, and household plumbing can add lead and copper to even treated water.

What to test

ParameterWhyHow often
Total coliform & E. coliSewage/animal contaminationYearly + after floods
NitrateFertiliser, septic; dangerous for infantsYearly
Lead & copperOld pipes, solder, fixturesOnce, then if plumbing changes
Arsenic, uranium, manganeseNatural geologyOnce, then every 3–5 years
PFASFirefighting foam, industry, landfillsIf near known sources
pH, hardness, ironCorrosion, scale, stainingEvery few years

How to sample

  1. Order sterile bottles from an accredited or certified lab.
  2. For bacteria, remove aerators, disinfect the tap, run water 2–3 minutes and fill without touching the inside of the cap.
  3. For lead, collect a first-draw sample after water has sat 6+ hours.
  4. Keep samples cold and deliver within the stated holding time.

Reading results

Compare each result with the maximum contaminant level (MCL) or guideline value in your jurisdiction. Any E. coli detection means the water is unsafe to drink until disinfected and re-tested.
